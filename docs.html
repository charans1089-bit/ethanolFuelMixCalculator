SCRK Engineering Brief v2.7

SCRK Motorsport Telemetry & Flex Fuel Architecture

A precision volumetric mixing calculator, seasonal compliance planner, and live Accessport telemetry diagnostic suite engineered for high-performance turbocharged direct-injection platforms.

01 Platform Overview & Philosophy

Precision Engineering · Volumetric Mixing · Tuner Safety

The SCRK Flex Fuel Mix Platform is built to solve a critical challenge in modern motorsport and modified street tuning: achieving consistent, repeatable ethanol targets without costly over-fueling, leaning out under high boost, or degrading sensitive direct-injection fuel system components.

Ethanol offers exceptional anti-knock characteristics (effective octane ratings in excess of 100–105 RON+MON/2) and tremendous charge-cooling latent heat of vaporization. However, pump E85 varies drastically by season (ranging between 54% in winter to 85%+ in summer) and standard pump 93 octane contains up to 10% ethanol (E10). The SCRK platform dynamically accounts for residual tank volume, current tank ethanol concentration, and supplier pump variances to deliver exact pump gallon prescriptions.

💡
Core Engineering Objective
Deliver exact, reproducible blends (such as E30, E45, E60, or E75+) within a tight tolerance of ±1.0% ethanol, eliminating the guesswork that often leads to inconsistent ignition timing or unexpected fuel trim compensations.

03 Mathematical Formulation & Conservation Laws

Volumetric Mass Balance · Multi-Source Ethanol Solution

The calculator operates on the physical principle of Conservation of Ethanol Volume under the assumption of ideal liquid mixing across gasoline and ethanol hydrocarbons.

General Mixing Equation
V_total · E_target = (V_cur · E_cur) + (V_e85 · E_pump85) + (V_93 · E_pump93)

Where \(V_{ ext{total}} = V_{ ext{cur}} + V_{ ext{e85}} + V_{93}\).

Solving for Planned E85 and 93 Octane Gallons

Let \(V_{ ext{added}} = V_{ ext{total}} - V_{ ext{cur}}\) represent the total capacity being filled. Substituting \(V_{93} = V_{ ext{added}} - V_{ ext{e85}}\) into the conservation formula yields the closed-form solution for required E85 gallons:

Exact Analytical Solution for E85 Gallons (V_e85)
V_e85 = [ V_total · E_target - V_cur · E_cur - V_added · E_pump93 ] / [ E_pump85 - E_pump93 ]

Once \(V_{ ext{e85}}\) is computed, the required 93 pump gas is immediately determined:

Top-Off 93 Octane Gallons (V_93)
V_93 = V_added - V_e85
⚠️
Physical Boundary Conditions & Tank Traps
If the requested target ethanol \(E_{ ext{target}}\) exceeds the mathematical maximum achievable with remaining fuel (i.e. \(V_{ ext{e85}} > V_{ ext{added}}\)), the application enters the Trap Simulator advisory mode, warning the driver of physical tank constraints.

05 Pit Stop Protocol & Fueling Sequence

Pump E85 First · Mechanical Mixing Dynamics · Nozzle Precision
Step Action Engineering Justification
Step 1 Pump Planned E85 First E85 has a higher specific density (~0.785 g/cm³) than pure gasoline (~0.740 g/cm³). Pumping E85 first deposits the denser fuel at the base of the tank.
Step 2 Top Off 93 Octane Second The high-flow turbulence of the subsequent 93 octane fill thoroughly agitates and mixes the fuel throughout the tank baffles and fuel pump basket.
Step 3 Verify on Cobb Accessport Drive 1.5–3.0 miles in light vacuum cruise to cycle the fuel line volume through the high-pressure fuel rail until the in-line ethanol sensor stabilizes.

06 High-Ethanol Safeguards & 3–4 Fill Rule

HPFP Lubricity · Hygroscopic Moisture Management · Detergent Washing

While ethanol delivers exceptional octane benefits, prolonged continuous operation at extreme ethanol concentrations (E75 to E85+) presents unique mechanical considerations for direct injection engines:

🚨
The 3–4 Consecutive Fill Rule (E75+ Advisory)
Tuner advisory recommends limiting consecutive E75+ fills to a maximum of 3 to 4 fill cycles. After reaching this threshold, run one full tank of top-tier 93 pump gas (or E10/E20 mild blend) before returning to high-ethanol blends.

Why the 93 Octane Cleaning Run is Critical

  1. High Pressure Fuel Pump Lubrication: Gasoline contains specialized lubricating hydrocarbons that protect the tight tolerances of the HPFP plunger and cam follower. Pure ethanol has lower lubricity.
  2. Detergent Fuel Additives: Top-tier 93 octane gasoline contains deposit-control detergents (polyetheramines) that wash away organic injector gumming and clean intake valve backsides.
  3. Moisture & Hygroscopic Scavenging: Ethanol naturally absorbs atmospheric moisture over time. A periodic tank of 93 gasoline displaces accumulated moisture in the tank sump.

08 Weather & Seasonal Volatility Compliance

ASTM D5798 Standards · Vapor Pressure · Cold Start Protection

Pump ethanol blends change across the calendar year to maintain adequate Reid Vapor Pressure (RVP) for reliable cold starting during winter months:

  • Summer (Class 1 / June–August): Minimum 79% to 85% ethanol concentration. Maximum power potential, highest knock suppression.
  • Transition (Class 2 / Spring & Fall): 70% to 79% ethanol. Excellent balance of power and ignition stability.
  • Winter (Class 3 & 4 / November–March): 54% to 70% ethanol blended with high-volatility winter gasoline to prevent hard cold-starts.

The Seasonal Blend Planner module in the SCRK suite calculates compliance against your local meteorological temperature profiles, suggesting target blend schedules customized to your region.
